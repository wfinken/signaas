Victorian
description: Nineteenth-century correspondence, unnecessarily obliged.
aliases: regency, edwardian, obedient-servant

I trust this finds you in tolerable health.
Forgive the liberty of this correspondence.
I have the honour to be, most sincerely,
Pray convey my regards to the household.
It would gratify me exceedingly to hear from you.
I am, as ever, at your disposal.
Let us not speak of the postage.
With the very warmest esteem,
I shall await your favour by the next post.
Believe me, I remain entirely yours,
Should the weather permit, I shall call on Thursday.
I beg you will excuse the brevity of this note.
It is a small matter, and yet I could not rest.
I dare not presume further upon your patience.
Written in haste, and with great feeling,
Yours in the deepest respect,
I remain, sir or madam, your most obedient servant, | {name}
Your humble correspondent, | {name}, Esq.
